120-day contribution plan · Director, AI Operations

How I would contribute

Read the work.
Earn the right to standardize.

This plan installs a useful central mechanism without assuming access to undisclosed Ontra workflows. Each phase produces evidence, capability, and explicit decisions - not a ceremonial transformation program.

01

Tactical empathy first

Understand what current work protects before prescribing change.

02

Value before scale

Start with workflow outcomes, not a catalog of tools.

03

Authority stays visible

Named humans retain interpretation, commitments, and exceptions.

04

Learning compounds

Every case records what should transfer and what must remain local.

01

Days 1-30

Read the work

Build tactical empathy for the workflows, constraints, and expertise already in place.

  • Interview department leaders, workflow owners, embedded practitioners, Engineering, Security, Procurement, Legal, Finance, and People partners.
  • Inventory active automations, approved tools, shadow use, vendor commitments, training needs, and escalation paths.
  • Observe representative work directly and separate workflow problems from tool requests.
  • Baseline decision clarity, preparation effort, rework, user confidence, retained use, quality, and risk signals.
Phase deliverableCurrent-state opportunity and boundary map, stakeholder topology, tool inventory, and discovery hypotheses.
02

Days 31-60

Establish useful boundaries

Create a lightweight operating system that helps teams execute instead of sending every decision to a central committee.

  • Launch an opportunity intake capturing work, value, owner, authority, boundary, adoption, and evidence.
  • Define intervention patterns and escalation rules with accountable partners.
  • Curate practical training by job context and establish advocate expectations.
  • Launch a working Community of Practice centered on active cases.
Phase deliverableAI Operations charter, intake and review cadence, first training pathways, and initial bounded precedent portfolio.
Operating ruleCentralize the mechanism. Keep judgment and outcomes with accountable workflow owners.

03

Days 61-90

Prove working precedents

Run interventions close to the work, instrument the experience, and make explicit reuse, local, repair, or stop decisions.

  • Co-design workflow changes with department owners and users.
  • Test prompts, automations, integrations, review steps, and exception paths inside approved boundaries.
  • Capture outcome, effort, quality, trust, adoption, and user-feedback evidence.
  • Document reusable core, legitimate local variation, unresolved risk, and the next decision.
  • Update guidance and training from observed behavior rather than theory.
Phase deliverableEvidence-backed operating precedents, user feedback, intervention decisions, and updated enablement.
04

Days 91-120

Institutionalize learning

Turn successful work into company capability while rationalizing tools, strengthening advocates, and shaping the next roadmap from evidence.

  • Publish a searchable precedent library and reusable decision guidance.
  • Establish an executive scorecard and portfolio review cadence.
  • Strengthen advocates with role clarity, examples, coaching, and feedback loops.
  • Identify tool/vendor consolidation, integration, security, or lifecycle decisions.
  • Present the next roadmap based on evidence, strategic importance, and change capacity.
Phase deliverablePrecedent library, scorecard, operating review, advocate network, tool/vendor recommendations, and next roadmap.

Balanced scorecard

Business outcome

Workflow effort, cycle time, completeness, rework, or decision latency.

Adoption

Retained use, owner participation, training usefulness, and advocate activity.

Trust & quality

Exceptions, corrections, review confidence, and boundary adherence.

Economics

Cost to maintain, vendor overlap, support burden, and time-to-value.

Learning speed

Time from signal to bounded proof and from proof to disposition.

Reuse

Patterns adapted by a second team without hiding legitimate variation.

Operating cadence

Weekly: active-case review and blocker resolution.
Monthly: portfolio, tool/vendor, adoption, and evidence review.
Quarterly: roadmap, precedent library, capability gaps, and executive decisions.

Decisions made clearer

What enters the portfolio; where AI Operations owns the mechanism; where departments retain authority; which tools and vendors remain; what scales; what stays local; what repairs; and what stops.
